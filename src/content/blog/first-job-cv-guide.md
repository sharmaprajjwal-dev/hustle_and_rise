A first-job CV is not expected to show a long employment history. Its job is to make your availability, relevant skills, and evidence of reliability easy to understand.

## Use a simple structure

Keep the document easy to scan:

1. Name and reliable contact details
2. A short profile tailored to the kind of role
3. Education and relevant training
4. Experience from work, volunteering, projects, clubs, or responsibilities
5. Skills supported by examples
6. References available on request, if appropriate

Avoid adding sensitive personal details such as your date of birth, passport number, or full street address.

## Turn responsibilities into evidence

Think beyond formal employment. Organising a group assignment can demonstrate planning. Helping at a community event can show customer service. Regularly caring for a family member can demonstrate responsibility and time management.

Write bullets that begin with an action and include context. For example: “Coordinated deadlines for a four-person class project and submitted the final presentation on time.”

## Tailor the top third

Read the job listing and identify the few requirements you can honestly support. Reflect those priorities in your profile and first bullets. Do not copy claims you cannot prove.

## Check the final document

Use consistent headings, dates, punctuation, and spacing. Save a PDF unless the employer requests another format. Give the file a clear name, such as `First-Last-CV.pdf`, and ask someone you trust to check it before sending.

> A short CV with clear evidence is stronger than a crowded CV filled with vague claims.
