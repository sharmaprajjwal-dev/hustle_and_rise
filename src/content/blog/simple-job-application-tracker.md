A useful application tracker answers three questions quickly: What have I applied for? What needs action next? What am I learning from the results?

## Keep the fields small

Start with a spreadsheet or table containing:

- Role and company
- Link to the original listing
- Location and work type
- Closing date, if supplied
- Date applied
- Current stage
- Next action and date
- Contact name, if you have one
- Notes

Avoid collecting information you will never use. The tracker should reduce mental load, not become another project.

## Use consistent stages

A short set of stages makes the list easy to filter: considering, preparing, applied, interview, offer, closed, and withdrawn. Update the stage whenever something changes.

## Add a weekly review

Once a week, close expired opportunities, schedule genuine follow-ups, and look for patterns. If suitable applications receive no responses, review how closely your CV matches the requirements. If interviews do not progress, focus your practice on the questions that caused difficulty.

Store only information you are entitled to keep, and avoid recording unnecessary personal details about recruiters or interviewers.

> The best tracker is the smallest one that helps you take the right next action.
